Incident intake and AI-assisted Clery classification

Reports arrive from the online form, CSA submissions and the police records system. The assistant highlights the facts behind each suggestion, scores its confidence, and routes the case to a person. Select a report.

Review queue: built to avoid false negatives

CSU's highest priority is not missing a reportable incident. The assistant never closes a case as "not Clery-reportable" by itself. Move the threshold: every case with any reportable signal below it goes to a second reviewer, not just the low-confidence positives.

Daily crime log

Entries are drafted from confirmed cases and must be posted within two business days. A records officer approves each entry; every edit is kept in the history.

Timely warning identification

The assistant watches for serious or continuing threats (for example, a series of similar crimes) and drafts a warning. It cannot send anything. Sending requires approval by the designated official, and the draft excludes victim names by design.

Safeguards

  • Evidence-linked: every suggestion cites the sentences it relied on; no citation, no suggestion.
  • Hallucination control: the model may only use facts in the case file and the Clery handbook rules; unsupported answers are blocked and flagged.
  • Confidence scoring: calibrated on a held-out labelled test set; low or conflicting scores trigger second review.
  • No early stop: a case stays open for re-review when new incidents or supplements are added.
  • No training on CSU data without CSU's prior written authorization; data stays in the United States.
  • Bias checks: quarterly review of overrides by category and geography.
